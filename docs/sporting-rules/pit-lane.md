# Pit Lane Procedure and Pit Loss

Doc-Id: sr-pitlane-001
Keywords: pit lane, pit loss, pit entry, pit exit, stationary time, pit crew

Every pit stop costs a car track position measured in lost time, commonly
called "pit loss." Pit loss is made up of four components, each of which
Pit Wall Copilot's strategy engine models separately and adds together
exactly once, on the lap the stop occurs:

1. **Pit entry** — the reduced-speed transit from the racing line into
   the pit lane, governed by a mandatory pit-lane speed limit.
2. **Lane transit** — travelling the length of the pit lane itself at the
   speed limit, which is far slower than racing speed.
3. **Stationary time** — the time the car is physically stopped at its
   pit box while the crew changes tires (and performs any other permitted
   work). This is the component most directly under the crew's control.
4. **Pit exit** — the reduced-speed transit back onto the racing line and
   the acceleration back up to full pace.

A driver who has just left the pits is also on "in-lap" tires that have
not yet reached their working temperature; the first lap or so after a
stop is measurably slower than subsequent laps on the same set, an effect
referred to as tire warm-up loss.
